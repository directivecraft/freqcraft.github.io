FreqCraft — a modern alternative to BrainWave Generator (BWGen)

BrainWave Generator was a Windows 95/98/NT binaural-beat and tone generator, last updated in 2005. It still runs for some — and crashes for many — on Windows 10 and 11. FreqCraft is a maintained successor, built for devices that are actually supported.

If you found this page, you already know what BWGen did: set a binaural beat frequency, choose a carrier tone and waveform, and run it as a precise, repeatable signal. FreqCraft does that on hardware from this decade — every Hz visible and editable, no shareware nag, no expired-evaluation lockout, no 2005 exception dialog.

Available now

FreqCraft (Android)

A precision binaural-beat and isochronic-tone generator you can run on your phone today, with up to five simultaneous voices — each with its own carrier, beat, waveform and level envelope. Every frequency editable to the decimal.

In development

FreqCraft Studio (desktop)

The full desktop successor to BWGen: unlimited voices, timed session sequencing, waveform harmonic filtering, layered noise and backgrounds, and .wav export. The remaining gap, closed.

Why people are moving off BrainWave Generator

These are not opinions — they come straight from the software itself:

  • It crashes on modern Windows. On current systems BWGen throws an internal exception (bwmain.c 3.1.12: 1361) and asks you to email a bug report to an address that has been dormant for years.
  • It was built for Windows 95, 98 and NT. Version 3.1 dates to 2005. There is no 64-bit build, no mobile version, and no maintenance.
  • The evaluation copy locks out. After one month it displays "EXPIRED UNREGISTERED EVALUATION COPY" and disables import until you pay the $40 shareware fee to a company that is no longer active.
  • Hardware assumptions are dated. Its headline visual feature drives AudioStrobe goggles — patent-restricted hardware almost nobody owns today.

The engine idea was sound; the delivery is 20 years past its platform. FreqCraft starts you on the core precision generator today, with the full desktop successor in development.
