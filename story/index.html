The experiment that built FreqCraft.

One story is not evidence. It is the reason a person builds a tool, which is a different thing, and worth telling straight.

Math was always my weak spot. I failed pre-algebra twice and barely made it through high school. Years later I learned there’s a name for it: dyscalculia, a numerical processing disorder, essentially dyslexia for numbers.

In 2012, in my mid-forties, I went back to college to take chemistry. The prerequisite was algebra, so I started at the bottom and clawed my way through, failing, retaking, scraping a C the hard way. I finally made it into the chemistry class. By the end of the semester I was on the edge of failing that one too.

That’s when I tried something different. With a documented-disability accommodation, I spent the final review weeks sitting in class every day wearing headphones, listening to a binaural-beats focus program on loop. No notes. No studying at home. Just the audio and the instructor working problems on the board.

The final exam was 28 pages. My accommodation gave me a five-hour window. I worked the way I always did, jumping around, skipping what stopped me, cycling back, ran thirty minutes over, and still turned it in unfinished.

98%
Final exam score

A student who’d been failing all semester, who handed in an incomplete exam, scored 98%. I can’t fully explain it. But it sent me down a path I never left.

FreqCraft is the tool I wish I’d had back then: transparent, precise, no black boxes, no subscriptions, no guesswork. Every Hz visible, every parameter yours to control. I’m not claiming it will do for you what that semester did for me. Everyone’s different, and this is an instrument for exploration, not a treatment. I built it because I lived something I couldn’t ignore, and I wanted a clean, honest tool to keep exploring it.

One story is not evidence. So there is an open protocol, a free, step-by-step method for testing auditory entrainment against your own EEG, with a stated null result and a standing invitation to publish whatever you find. You do not need FreqCraft to run it.
